\begin{abstract}
UAV RGB-thermal alignment is a prerequisite for reliable aerial inspection, monitoring, and
nighttime multimodal perception, yet it remains difficult to make a scene-level alignment decision
from real cross-FOV aerial imagery. Modern multimodal matchers can already produce abundant
pairwise correspondences, but viewpoint variation, cross-resolution sensing, thermal rendering
changes, and low-light imagery make the resulting frame-level homographies uneven in quality. This
reveals a gap between pairwise match availability and scene-level alignment reliability. We present
UAV-TAlign, a scene-level RGB-thermal alignment framework that turns strong pairwise multimodal
matching into reliable scene-level decisions through reliability-guided evidence selection, robust
homography consensus, and QA-aware verification. We further package UAV-TAlign-1K, a public
evaluation subset with a label-free protocol for studying real UAV RGB-thermal alignment without
requiring manual landmark annotation as a prerequisite. Experiments against classical and modern
off-the-shelf baselines, protocol-consistency analysis, and cumulative module ablations show that
the decisive gains come from scene-level consensus and reliability-aware verification rather than
from pairwise matcher availability alone.

\keywords{RGB-thermal registration \and UAV imagery \and multimodal alignment \and scene-level
reliability}
\end{abstract}
