\section{Results}

\subsection{Pairwise Baseline Comparison}

We begin by asking whether the main difficulty on UAV-TAlign-1K still lies in obtaining usable
pairwise RGB-thermal correspondences. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise_baselines} shows that this is no
longer the dominant bottleneck. Modern off-the-shelf multimodal methods already provide highly
saturated pairwise usability, with raw MINIMA and RoMa both reaching 500/500 usable pairwise
registrations, LoFTR reaching 499/500 under a resize-aware inference setting, and XoFTR-640
reaching 494/500. This result motivates the main design of UAV-TAlign: the framework targets the
scene-level reliability layer that remains after strong pairwise matching is available.

\subsection{Scene-Level Performance of UAV-TAlign}

The near-saturated pairwise picture motivates the scene-level reliability layer. Under the
canonical QA-aware criterion, UAV-TAlign retains 7 of 15 scenes with a mean accepted-frame ratio of
64.5\%. This reliability-controlled operating point emphasizes high-confidence scene transforms
and highlights why robust consensus and verification are valuable on top of modern pairwise
matchers.

\subsection{Condition Breakdown}

The scene and condition breakdowns show that QA-aware scene decisions are structured rather than
uniformly distributed. Night scenes are the most demanding group under the canonical criterion,
while low-light scenes include successful retained alignments. These patterns support
the view that the bottleneck is scene-level reliability rather than pairwise matcher availability
alone.

\subsection{Protocol and Proxy Evidence}

We further examine whether the stored QA and proxy signals are consistent with the canonical
scene-level decisions. The strongest discriminator is the reliability signal
\texttt{baseline\_relative\_qa\_outliers}, which separates all 8 QA-filtered scenes from the 7
retained scenes. Accepted ratio is also informative as part of the joint decision: scene 02 has
98\% accepted frames but is QA-filtered because reject and QA-relative outlier signals remain high,
whereas scene 04 is retained with 80\% accepted frames when these signals are absent. These
observations support the claim that UAV-TAlign captures scene-level reliability rather than merely
counting matched frames.

\centering

\subsection{Cumulative Ablation}

The cumulative ablations show that the clearest empirical gains come from robust scene consensus
and QA-aware verification. Adding robust consensus substantially improves edge and gradient
alignment proxies while reducing severe outliers. Adding QA-aware verification then converts the
line from an accepted-frame count into a reliability-controlled scene-level criterion, matching the
formal 8-scene slice at 5/8 retained scenes.

same fixed subset. We therefore present deterministic-even selection as a reproducible default
operating policy, while robust consensus and QA-aware verification remain the primary empirical
drivers.
