\section{Introduction}

RGB-thermal alignment is becoming a foundational capability for UAV inspection, monitoring, and
nighttime multimodal perception. Visible imagery provides semantic and structural detail, while
thermal imagery remains informative under illumination changes, smoke, haze, and low-light
conditions. This complementarity has driven progress across multispectral pedestrian perception,
thermal object detection, visible-thermal tracking, and UAV multimodal sensing
\cite{hwang2015kaist,flir2018thermal,jia2021llvip,liu2022tardal,sun2022dronevehicle,zhang2022vtuav,li2022lasher,jiang2023antiuav}.
For these systems to operate as fused sensors rather than separate streams, however, RGB and
thermal observations must be aligned reliably at the scene level.

Real UAV RGB-thermal alignment is substantially more demanding than standard same-view image
matching. Aerial platforms introduce viewpoint drift, cross-FOV capture, rolling scale changes, and
cross-resolution sensors; thermal images further introduce weak texture, grayscale or pseudocolor
rendering, and strong day/night appearance shifts. These factors make individual frame-level
homographies uneven: some pairs are well supported, while others are geometrically unstable or
visually plausible but unreliable. The practical question is therefore not only whether a matcher
can produce correspondences for one pair, but whether a system can decide which scene-level
alignment should be trusted.

Recent pairwise matching has advanced rapidly. Transformer-based, dense, and multimodal matchers
such as LoFTR, LightGlue, DKM, RoMa, XoFTR, and MINIMA have substantially improved correspondence
availability under difficult appearance changes
\cite{sarlin2020superglue,sun2021loftr,chen2022aspanformer,lindenberger2023lightglue,edstedt2023dkm,edstedt2024roma,tuzcuoglu2024xoftr,ren2025minima}.
Our experiments confirm this progress in the UAV RGB-thermal setting: strong off-the-shelf
multimodal matchers already achieve near-saturated pairwise usability on UAV-TAlign-1K. Yet the
same result also exposes a new bottleneck. Pairwise correspondences are often available, but
available correspondences do not automatically yield a reliable scene-level alignment. The missing
piece is a reliability layer that aggregates evidence across a scene, suppresses unstable
homographies, and verifies whether the final transform should be retained.

This observation motivates a different framing of the problem. Instead of treating UAV
RGB-thermal registration as another pairwise matching benchmark, we study it as a scene-level
reliability problem. Under this view, modern matchers are powerful evidence generators,
but the alignment algorithm must still answer three system-level questions: which frames should
contribute evidence, how should multiple homographies be fused into a scene consensus, and when is
the resulting transform reliable enough to use? This framing is especially important for UAV data,
where retaining an unstable scene-level transform can be more harmful than conservatively
withholding one.

We present UAV-TAlign, a scene-level UAV RGB-thermal alignment framework designed around this
reliability layer. UAV-TAlign complements modern pairwise matchers rather than replacing them. It
uses reliability-guided evidence selection to obtain representative frame support, robust
homography consensus to consolidate frame-level estimates, and QA-aware verification to retain only
scene-level transforms that satisfy conservative reliability criteria. In this sense, UAV-TAlign
elevates strong pairwise multimodal matching into a deployable scene-level alignment decision.

We also package UAV-TAlign-1K, a public evaluation subset for real UAV RGB-thermal alignment. The
subset contains 15 scenes and 500 RGB-thermal pairs spanning day, night, low-light, grayscale
thermal, pseudocolor thermal, and wide/zoom scene conditions. Because dense manual geometric
annotation is costly and can be ambiguous in weak-texture thermal regions, we accompany the subset
with a label-free protocol that evaluates pairwise usability, scene-level retention, and
reliability diagnostics. The protocol is not positioned as a replacement for absolute geometric
ground truth; rather, it provides a practical and reproducible way to study scene-level alignment
reliability under real UAV RGB-thermal conditions.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce UAV-TAlign, a scene-level UAV RGB-thermal alignment framework that turns strong
pairwise multimodal matching into reliable scene-level decisions through
reliability-guided evidence selection, robust homography consensus, and QA-aware verification.
\item We package UAV-TAlign-1K, a public UAV RGB-thermal evaluation subset with a label-free
protocol for studying real cross-FOV aerial alignment without requiring manual landmark annotation
as a prerequisite.
\item Through strong off-the-shelf baselines, reliability diagnostics, and cumulative module
ablations, we show that the dominant empirical gains arise from scene-level consensus and
verification rather than pairwise matcher availability alone.
\end{itemize}
