\section{Discussion}

The current results suggest that pairwise matcher availability is no longer the primary bottleneck
on UAV-TAlign-1K. Strong off-the-shelf multimodal matchers already succeed on most individual
pairs, yet scene-level canonical passes remain far from saturated. This shift in the bottleneck
motivates UAV-TAlign as a reliability-oriented pipeline built around candidate selection,
aggregation, and QA-aware decision logic rather than as a new local matcher.

The present evidence is strong enough to support the paper's main practical message, but it does
not eliminate all uncertainty. In particular, the current proxy analysis supports the internal
logic of the protocol without replacing manual geometric ground truth, and the method should still
be presented as a practical and reproducible pipeline rather than a final solution to UAV
RGB-thermal registration.

The completed multi-seed random-selection supplement also suggests that deterministic selection is
best treated as a stable operating policy rather than a universal performance-superiority claim.
Under the current evidence hierarchy, the strongest supported gains come from robust aggregation and
QA-aware scene decision.

\subsection{Current Limitations}

The current paper version still has several clear limits. First, the evaluation protocol is
label-free rather than based on manual geometric ground truth. Second, the scene-level model is
still based on homography-style alignment, which can be stressed by stronger parallax or more
complex non-planar structure. Third, although UAV-TAlign-1K is sufficient for the present study, it
is a focused public subset rather than a large fully annotated benchmark.
